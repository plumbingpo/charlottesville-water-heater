Keeping Your Gordonsville Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so you can reach us fast the moment something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
