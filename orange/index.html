How Orange Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a surprise cleanup.

Hot Water Headaches Orange Homeowners Face

Long before a tank floods a Orange garage, it almost always shows warning signs. Look out for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or showers that run cold sooner every month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Ignored, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so small problems stay small.
