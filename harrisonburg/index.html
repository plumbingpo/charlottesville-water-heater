Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Harrisonburg

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush every year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
