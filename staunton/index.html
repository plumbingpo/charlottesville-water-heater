For a leak, close the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then cut the power at the breaker on an electric model or turn the gas knob to "off" on a gas model. Clear stored items away from the heater and lay towels around the base to keep the water contained. Never ignore the smell of gas: get everyone out and call the gas utility from a safe spot.

Keeping Your Staunton Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care is the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a yearly test of the T&P valve, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and also shows rusty water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

In Staunton, we work on every kind of water heater, and each one has a typical way of failing. Gas tank heaters usually give trouble through thermocouples and flame sensors, dirty burners, and draft problems in the flue. Electric units lose heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment buries the lower element. Tankless units throw error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor problems, most of which trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
